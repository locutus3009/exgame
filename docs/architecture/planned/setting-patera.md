# The Pater System — setting description

> **Document status.** Working draft. Records what has been agreed in
> discussion so far. Open questions are marked `[OPEN]`; decisions not yet
> final are marked `[TENT.]` (tentative).
> Epochs and the history of Terra are in section 6-bis. The story's "present"
> = **the literal beginning of the space age**.

> **SETTING FOCUS.** This is a setting about **the development of an
> interplanetary civilization in a rich young system**. The mystery of origin
> (the seeding) is **one of several equal facets and drivers**, a deep
> background detail, and **NOT** the central mechanism or "the secret
> everything leads to". The setting works fully even if the origin is never
> revealed. The drivers are equal (see section 11): settling the system,
> resources, the society/politics of a young spacefaring civilization, Chione
> as a second home, Frater, and — on equal footing — the riddle of the seeding
> and the Ark.
>
> **The genre is not fixed** and the setting is neutral toward it: it fits a
> strategy game, a space sim, a sandbox (KSP-style), an RPG. Hence it rests on
> **space and systems**, not on a single storyline.

## 0. Code names

| Name | Object | Origin of the name |
|-----|--------|---------------------|
| **Pater** | Pseudo-Saturn in the habitable zone, gas giant | Lat. "father" |
| **Terra** | Pater's habitable moon | Lat. "earth" |
| **Rhea**, **Io** | Pater's two large regular moons | working names |
| **Eos** | Super-Venus on a close orbit | Gk. "dawn" |
| **Frater** | Distant super-Jupiter | Lat. "brother" (pair to Pater) |
| **Chione** | Cold super-Earth at 3–4 AU | Gk. goddess of snow |

Logic of the pantheon: Pater (father giant, warm) and Frater (brother giant,
distant and cold) are the two giants; Eos (the near hellworld), Terra (home),
Chione (the cold "sister" world).

## 1. The star and overall architecture

### The star `[decided]`
- Class **G6–G8V**, luminosity **L ≈ 0.7 L☉**, mass **M ≈ 0.92 M☉**,
  T_eff ~5500 K.
- **System age ≈ 2 Gyr** — young. The star is more active than the Sun:
  faster rotation, more flares, denser stellar wind.
- Habitable zone: center ~0.84 AU (√0.7), conservative belt ~0.7–1.0 AU.
- **Snow line ≈ 2.3 AU** (∝ √L, shifted inward relative to the solar one).

### Radial layout `[decided / partly open]`
From the star outward:

1. **mini-Mercury** ~0.1–0.2 AU
2. **Eos** (semi-molten world) ~0.3–0.4 AU
3. **Pater** (gas giant) **0.85 AU** + moon system
4. swept-out corridor + thin inner belt (0.85–2.5 AU)
5. snow line (~2.3 AU)
6. **Chione** (cold super-Earth) ~3–4 AU, in a protected corridor
7. outer belt (resonances with Frater)
8. **Frater** (super-Jupiter) ~5–7 AU + rich icy retinue
9. **Gatekeeper ice giant** ~15 AU (Frater's stunted twin)
10. giant trojans (L4/L5), truncated outer edge (no scattered disk)
11. **Companion star** (early K) at ~100–200 AU + its poor system

**Key principle for filling the space "between the giants":** Pater migrated
via Type II from ~2.5–3 AU inward to 0.85 AU. It ate out the zone **inward**
of its birthplace (the 0.85–2.5 AU corridor is poor). It never crossed the
space **outward** of ~2.5 AU — there is plenty of room there for whole planets
(Chione) and belts.

## 2. Pater (the gas giant) and its system

### Pater itself `[decided]`
- Saturn class, **~0.3 M_J** (~0.6–1.0 Saturn masses), radius R_p ~60,000 km.
- Orbit: a ≈ 0.85 AU, **e < 0.05** (critical for Terra's climate),
  axial tilt (obliquity) ~25° → gives Terra seasons and "eclipse seasons".
- Self-luminous in the IR from residual heat of formation (youth of the
  system), but by 2 Gyr the internal heat has nearly faded — **it does not
  affect Terra's climate**.
- Hill radius at 0.85 AU ≈ 6 million km (~100 R_p).
- Zone of stable prograde orbits — out to **~40 R_p** (~0.4 R_Hill).
- The magnetosphere is compressed by the stellar wind (close to the star +
  active young star): magnetopause ~10 R_p (Saturn's is ~22). Radiation belts
  lie in the inner magnetosphere; a moon's habitable zone must lie outside the
  hard belts.

### Terra — the habitable moon `[decided]`

**Origin: early capture + growth by accretion.**
- A **planetesimal / rocky embryo** (0.05–0.2 M⊕) was captured during the gas
  epoch of Pater's formation. A light embryo is easy to catch (good gas drag +
  pull-down as the Hill sphere grows), onto a prograde coplanar orbit.
- It then **grew by accretion in orbit** to **0.6–0.8 M⊕** from circumplanetary
  disk material and from what Pater swept up during migration.
- This removes the statistical stretch of "catching a whole Earth-world": an
  embryo was caught, the mass was gained locally.

**What this origin provides (all for free):**
- Rocky (Earth-like) composition — an embryo from the inner zone, not ice.
- An early **strong dynamo** — accretional heating + late bombardment
  → hot differentiated metallic core → magnetic shielding from Pater's
  radiation and the stellar wind.
- An orbit that is coplanar and circular almost from birth; tides finished off
  the small residual inclination and e over 2 Gyr.
- Water: volatiles from the late bombardment → ocean (initially Archean,
  anoxic).

**Orbit and rotation:**
- a ≈ **12 R_p** (~0.72 million km), at the outer edge of the regular retinue.
- **Tidally locked 1:1** (synchronous rotation) — the only stable state; a 3:2
  spin-orbit state is impossible (it needs e≈0.2, which breaks the climate and
  melts the moon with tides).
- Orbital period around Pater ≈ **7 Earth days** = Terra's solar day
  (~3.5 days of light / ~3.5 days of dark).
- In a **3:2 orbital mean-motion resonance** with an inner moon (~9 R_p). The
  resonance maintains a small e → feeds tidal heating → the dynamo.

**Terra's mass dominates the system:** 0.6–0.8 M⊕ versus ~0.01–0.015 M⊕ for
Rhea/Io. Terra is gravitationally dominant, which is why it sits at the outer
edge of the retinue (it would have scattered large neighbors). More on Terra
in section 6.

### Rhea and Io — regular moons `[decided]`
- Pater's own **rocky** moons, formed from its disk.
- **Moon class or slightly larger** (~0.01–0.015 M⊕) — **smaller than they
  could have been**: the growing dominant (Terra) starved them. See the
  "price of Terra" section.
- **Io** is tidally heated (resonant chain), **lava-covered**, sulfur-volcanic,
  like the real Io. Inner orbits.
- Terra's 3:2 partner sits at ~9 R_p and is massive enough for the resonance to
  hold (Rhea class is the lower bound for a stable partner).

### Small moons `[decided]`
- **Many small rocky** bodies (tens to hundreds of km). Ring shepherds,
  resonant small fry.
- **Not a single icy moon** — ice is unstable in the habitable zone (see
  below). The system is **dry**, apart from Terra's bound water.

### The irregular sentinel `[decided]`
- One **captured rocky dwarf planet of Rhea class** (~1000–1500 km) on an
  inclined, **possibly retrograde** orbit at ~30 R_p.
- Cheap, stable, does not perturb Terra, rocky (no ice).
- "Do not overuse" — one such object, no more.
- In Terra's sky: a wandering point of ~2–4 arcmin on a slanting path,
  sometimes resolvable as a disk; distinct from the small fry moving along the
  plane.

### Rings `[decided]`
- **Dim, dark, thin, refractory** (rock and dust, no ice).
- Main source: **debris of a destroyed rocky cinder moon** (as with the real
  Saturn — young rings from a moon that broke up).
  Replenishment: shepherds shedding at the Roche limit + impact ejecta.
- Inner edge ~2.4 R_p (Roche limit), outer ~3 R_p.

### "No ice" — the mechanism `[decided]`
- Pater was born beyond the snow line with a **normal icy retinue**
  (Saturnian type). During its inward migration to 0.85 AU (T ~230–275 K) the
  retinue **devolatilized**:
  - large icy moons shrank to rocky cores (~70% of the diameter);
  - small purely icy ones (Mimas/Enceladus class) evaporated entirely —
    **they do not exist**.
- The present rocky-and-lava population is the **devolatilized remnant** of a
  once-icy system, systematically shrunk and densified.
- Consequence: no ocean moons (Europas), no icy rings. The only wet world is
  Terra, and it is wet because of its **mass** (gravity + atmosphere retain the
  water), not because of cold.

### "The price of Terra" — the structure of the retinue `[decided]`
- The budget of the regular retinue is fixed by the planet's mass: ~10⁻⁴ M_Pater
  ≈ **0.01 M⊕** in total. Terra is **outside** this budget (captured + grown
  from outside), but while growing it **stripped** the local disk.
- Result: the large caliber "moved" into Terra → Rhea/Io are underfed, there
  are no mid-sized moons, a sharp drop-off into small fry.
- Structurally this is a **hyper-Saturn**: one dominant + a swarm, more
  lopsided than Saturn's (Saturn's Titan is a disk product; Pater's Terra is
  an interloper).
- **The poverty of large bodies = the diagnostic trace of capture.** It is a
  signature, not impoverishment.
- Compensation: the caliber left the retinue but remains in Terra's sky —
  Pater itself (a 9.5° disk) + an abundance of small fry (frequent transits,
  mini-eclipses).
- **One more large body does not fit the budget** (there is room, there is no
  mass). Only by capture — and only as the irregular sentinel above; a second
  Earth-world or a second regular Titan cannot exist.

## 3. Eos — the semi-molten world `[decided]`
- **An Earth-mass Terra that never was** (~0.9–1.5 M⊕): the mass is
  deliberately close to Terra's so the contrast reads as a fork **by
  distance**, not by mass.
- Orbit ~0.3–0.4 AU (inside the HZ, the "Venusian" belt), closer than Pater.
- Tipped into a **runaway greenhouse**: thick CO₂ atmosphere, hundreds of °C.
- **Semi-molten**, not a static Venus: a substellar region with
  softened/partly molten crust, silicate haze. (A full lava ocean only if moved
  to ~0.2 AU; at 0.3–0.4 it is "molten in places".)
- **Active hydrodynamic atmospheric escape** under the XUV of the young active
  star → an extended **tail** of escaping atmosphere, swept back by the stellar
  wind. Observable from Terra; tied to the youth of the system.
- **Role:** the hot pole of the climate triad (**Eos** too hot — **Terra**
  just right — **Chione** too cold). A vivid demonstration of what would have
  become of Terra half a step closer to the star. The senders saw
  spectroscopically that Eos had tipped over → a reason why Terra became the
  target.

## 4. Mini-Mercury `[decided — background]`
- A bare, scorched rocky/iron cinder, ~0.1–0.2 AU, a fraction of M⊕, probably
  tidally locked to the star (eternal day/night), dayside red-hot. Thin
  atmosphere or none.
- **Role — just an inner planet to complete the picture.** Not worked out
  seriously; carries no narrative weight (Eos holds the system's hot pole).

## 5. Chione — the cold super-Earth `[decided]`
> **Terminology caveat:** in *mood* it is a harsh, inhospitable "super-Mars";
> but physically it is an **Earth-mass world with a DENSE atmosphere**, not
> Martian thin-air desert. Martian conditions are caused by *low* mass (the
> atmosphere cannot be held) — Earth mass rules them out. "Mars" here is an
> image of harshness, not an atmosphere type.
- **Location:** ~3–4 AU, in the stability corridor between Pater and Frater
  (squeezed by the gravity of both → stable over the long term), just beyond
  the snow line. Heliocentric orbit — **an ordinary planet, not a capture**.
- **Mass:** ~1–1.5 M⊕ (Earth-mass; safely below the sub-Neptune threshold of
  ~2–5 M⊕). The mass is an asset: it will hold an atmosphere built during
  terraforming (unlike leaky Mars).
- **Origin and surface:** the key choice is an **accessible solid surface**.
  Achieved through:
  - **moderate mass** (≤~1.5 M⊕) — it did not grab an H/He envelope;
  - **late assembly** — it assembled after the gas dispersed, so there was
    nowhere for hydrogen to come from (like Earth → a secondary atmosphere,
    not a hydrogen one).
  - At 3–4 AU photoevaporation is weak — whatever stuck will not be blown off;
    so one cannot count on the core being "stripped bare". Protection only via
    mass/timing.
- **Composition:** rock-and-ice, but **with a notable fraction of crustal
  water/ice** (not dehydrated to zero — realistic beyond the snow line, and it
  removes the problem of delivering water for terraforming).
- **Climate now:** very cold (equilibrium T ~150–180 K), dense secondary
  atmosphere (CO₂/N₂/CH₄), **global glaciation**, a solid icy/rocky surface.

### Terraformability `[decided]`
- **Terraformable in principle, and EASIER than Mars.** Two independent tasks:
  - *Hold an atmosphere* — beats Mars outright: Earth mass
    (v_esc ~11 km/s) + a possible dynamo → a built atmosphere stays.
    Mars is fundamentally leaky; Chione is not.
  - *Warm it* — harder than Mars (far away, dim star), but this is an
    **engineering** task with levers, not a physical prohibition.
- **Biology alone does NOT start it:** photosynthesis pumps out CO₂ → cools
  (anti-greenhouse); life does not start in the frost (it needs liquid water
  for its own start — chicken and egg). Biology is an **amplifier**, not a
  starter.
- **Minimal recipe without mirrors:** (1) an abiotic starter — super-greenhouse
  gases (perfluorocarbons/SF₆, ppm); (2) evaporate the frozen-in
  CO₂/volatiles to ~1+ bar; (3) water / the water-vapor loop; (4) **then**
  biology — methanogens first (CH₄ warms), careful oxygenation later.
- The order of phases is **the reverse of Terra's**: on Terra oxygen came first
  (risk of freezing it out — a knife edge); on Chione you hold the greenhouse
  first, oxygen at the end.

### Role in the story `[decided]`
- **NOT the colonizer's target and NOT a seeding reserve.** The colonizer does
  only biology, not geoengineering (see section 7), and Chione requires
  **warming** → outside its mandate. It could not have used Chione.
- **Role — a growth target for the descendants (second act).** The
  descendants can already warm worlds (unlike the one-shot colonizer) → for
  them Chione is terraformable.
- **The system-selection factor sits with the senders, not the colonizer.**
  Selection criterion: **one ready seedable world (Terra) in a system with
  room to grow.** The richness of the system (Chione + the companion's planet
  + Frater's icy moons) is the senders' bet on the colony's **future**: a
  colony with room to expand is more likely to keep spreading life.
- Contrast with the Solar System: Chione is "a good reserve that we did not
  have" (Venus burned, Mars leaked; each failed one task, Chione passes both:
  mass = retention, volatiles = feedstock for warming).
- **State as of the "present" — UNTOUCHED `[decided]`.** The colonizer did not
  touch it (outside its mandate), and the descendants at the start of the
  space age have not yet reached it. A cold virgin world, the target of a
  **possible** future, not a current construction site.

## 6. Terra — in detail

### Tidal locking and the day `[decided]`
- 1:1 locking **to Pater** (not to the star!) → **no permanently dark
  hemisphere**: the sun moves across the sky over the whole surface on a
  ~7-day cycle.
- The near/far division is **only about the visibility of Pater**, not about
  light/temperature.

### Near-side / Far-side `[decided]` — geography sewn into the orbit
- **Near side:** Pater is always in the sky (~9.5° disk ≈ 19 full moons),
  nights are lit by reflected planetshine, **an eclipse once per "day"**
  (Terra dives into Pater's shadow) → regular hours-long "mini-winters".
- **Far side:** Pater is never visible, dark stable nights, no eclipses.
- This is a **ready-made line of cultural and climatic schism**, imprinted in
  the mechanics. Possibly — the seeders' starting populations were
  deliberately spread apart.

### Climate `[decided]`
- 0.7 L☉ + a suppressed greenhouse (see oxygenation) → a **cold, "Nordic"**
  world: broad polar caps, glaciated high latitudes, a **narrow** warm
  equatorial belt (a ribbon, not half the world).
- Slow rotation (7-day day) → weak Coriolis → broad, smeared circulation
  cells, shifted rain/desert belts, sluggish weather. The three-day night →
  strong nocturnal cooling outside the tropics → selection for frost
  hardiness.
- **The warmest belt runs across the tidal axis** → the best land is cut by
  longitude: sub-giant equator → flank sectors → anti-giant equator. A
  gradient of sky and climate along the valuable strip.
- Continents are placed by tectonics (freedom of placement). The centrifugal
  factor and the locking do **not** place continents (not on Earth either).
- Volcanic provinces are **patterned** by tidal heating (axisymmetric about
  the rotation axis) — not random; the only physical "magnet" of geology.
  Geothermal oases in the high-latitude ice are possible.
- **Tides:** Pater is fixed in the sky → its bulge is static (a permanent
  bulge, no cycle). Moving tides are raised by **the star** → slow solar
  tides, half-period ~3.5 days. Intertidal zones live on a slow weekly rhythm.

### Terra's sky `[decided]`
- Dominated by **Pater** (~9.5°, disk + rings edge-on / slightly open).
- **The rings "breathe":** a small inclination of Terra's orbit (fractions of a
  degree to a couple of degrees, a remnant of capture; the orbit is drawn
  toward the Laplace plane ≈ the ring plane) → over the ~7-day orbit the rings
  twice collapse into a thread (edge-on) and twice open into a thin dark
  ellipse. Half-period ~3.5 days.
- The rings' shadow on Pater's clouds; Pater's shadow on the rings.
- **The 3:2 partner** (Rhea class, ~9 R_p): its distance swings over the
  synodic period → the disk **pulsates** from ~0.4° (conjunction, almost our
  Moon) down to a point at opposition.
- **Io** (if visible) — a dim reddish disk with volcanic night glow.
- Small fry — frequent fast point transits and mini-eclipses.
- The irregular sentinel — a wandering point on a slanting orbit.
- **The dead Ark** — a point of magnitude ~+22 (see below), invisible to the
  eye.
- Three nested sky rhythms: the ~7-day diel, the eclipse rhythm (once per
  day), the seasonal one (~0.8 year, from Pater's axial tilt).

### Oxygenation (terraforming of Terra) `[decided]`
- Initially a sterile world, an Archean anoxic ocean.
- **Engineered oxygenation took ~hundreds of thousands of years** (up to ~1
  million with active volcanism). The limit is not the atmosphere (~10¹⁸ kg
  O₂) but the **sinks** (~10¹⁹ kg: dissolved Fe(II), reduced minerals, the
  flux of volcanic gases).
- Mechanism — **the Carboniferous model**: productive crops with durable
  structural tissue (lignin / an engineered biopolymer) + **decomposers
  deliberately not introduced** → carbon is buried without rotting. Oxygen
  accumulates because nothing returns the C to CO₂.
- What gets buried is **C-H-O, not N-P** (enhanced nutrient resorption from
  dying tissue) → the biosphere does not starve.
- Cold is an ally (boreal peatlands slow decomposition). Two regimes: cold
  peat bogs at high latitudes + warm shallow-water mangroves/marshes at the
  equator (blue carbon).
- **The carbon/greenhouse knife edge:** pumping out CO₂ for the sake of O₂
  cools an already dim world → oxygenation was carried out carefully and
  slowly → the resulting climate is **climatically marginal** (cold, large
  caps). The Nordic climate is a consequence of the oxygen budget, not a
  separate decision.
- An animal-free phase until oxygen built up (animals are net O₂ consumers and
  accelerate decomposition). Flora dispersed by spores, wind and water (the
  Carboniferous set), diazotrophs.
- As a side effect — methanogenesis in anoxic bogs (CH₄: both an O₂ sink and a
  greenhouse gas for warming).
- A biochemically terrestrial biosphere, rebuilt around the 7-day cycle
  (weekly sleep/activity rhythms in flora and fauna).

### Catalog and endemics `[decided]`
- **The biosphere is complete and rich:** the colonizer carried over
  **hundreds of thousands of species** — not a minimal set but an elaborated
  ecosystem with all tiers, including terrestrial vertebrates, **primates and
  human relatives**. The colonizer did its best.
- **In places it has already evolved locally.** Over ~1 million years under an
  alien sky (7-day cycle, Nordic climate, near/far, isolation in pockets) part
  of the catalog lineages **diverged into endemics** that never existed on
  Earth — especially fast-evolving groups: **new birds, insects**, small
  vertebrates. In some places the biosphere is familiarly terrestrial, in
  others it is already its own.
- Tone: the biosphere is not a "terrestrial museum" but **terrestrial,
  gone-wild and diverged** — recognizable at its base, but with alien
  branches. (This is also material for the paleontological paradox, see
  section 7: the tree is there, but there is no root beneath it.)

## 6-bis. Geology, landscape and history of Terra

### Young, rough coastal geology `[decided]`
- The biosphere is ~1 million years old → **no mature Earth-style beaches**.
  Modern beaches are the product of billions of years of reworking (quartz
  sand, shell carbonate). Terra has none of that.
- Coasts: exposed **basalt and granite**, angular unrounded debris, rock
  ledges, boulder fields. Sand is rare and "immature" — dark,
  volcanic/feldspathic (black beaches), not white quartz.
  **No carbonate beaches** (no shell/reef fauna in the early phase).
- **Giant peatlands** (on the scale of West Siberia) — the legacy of the dying
  oxygen forest in shallows and deltas. Impassable: quagmire, floating mats,
  acidic water, rotting wood.
- Usable land = **rare pockets** between two hostile shores (where it is soft —
  quagmire, where it is hard — rock). This is the **physical contour of
  isolation** that props up the pre-agrarian ceiling.
- **The sea is at first a wall, not a road:** cliffs without harbors + rough
  shallows → seafaring gets off to a hard start.
- The world's palette: dark basalt shores, black sand, rust-brown peatlands to
  the horizon, leaden shallows, white tundras and caps. A cold, dark,
  **immature** world — not a "green-and-blue postcard".

### Terraforming legacy = the material base of civilization `[decided]`
A running motif: the whole material history of Terra is a delayed echo of the
seeding.
- **Peat** → fuel (peat→coal); bog ore; and (once flooded) an inland sea (see
  below).
- **Oxygenation** → precipitation of dissolved Fe(II) → **banded iron
  formations (BIF)** in superabundance (as on Earth, but compressed into
  hundreds of thousands of years → thicker than Earth's).
- **Two-horizon biogenic iron:**
  - *Bog iron* from the peatlands — **low-melting**, a bloomery furnace handles
    it easily, ubiquitous → feeds an **early Iron Age**.
  - *BIF* — **refractory** (hematite/magnetite + silica), requires a blast
    furnace → a "sleeping giant" for the **industrial** phase.

### Historical periodization `[decided]`
1. **The pre-agrarian ceiling** — ~100 thousand years; cycles of
   languages-and-cultures, collapses of proto-sedentary settlements, isolation
   in pockets (see section 7).
2. **A climatic window** (warming) → **flooding of the peatlands**: the
   barriers drown, an **inland sea of islands and lagoons** appears + a burst
   of coastal productivity (flooding the peat releases nutrients → fish,
   shellfish).
3. **Thalassocracy** (flourishing ~**2–3 thousand years ago**): the first
   breakthrough is a **maritime trading** civilization, not an agrarian river
   one. Coastal shipping along sheltered waters linked the isolated pockets →
   specialization, surplus, **writing from trade accounting**, a **creole
   lingua franca**, merchant elites. Held together by network connectivity
   rather than fragile local farming.
   *A full analogue of classical antiquity — studied by archaeology/history,
   NOT living memory.*
4. **Cooling** → the window closed → **collapse of the thalassocracy** (the
   warm inland sea recedes, networks break, imported alloy metal becomes
   unavailable). Earth rhyme: the Bronze Age collapse.
5. **The continental Iron Age** of the successors → a switch to local **bog
   ore** → the center of gravity shifts **from the sea to the mainland**
   (the-Bronze-collapse-gave-birth-to-iron).
6. **Revival of seafaring through the NORTHERN seas** → oceanic
   colonization. The sea returns **from the other end** (after the cooling the
   north is more passable), from iron-using continental cultures →
   **Nordic** in origin, not an heir of the warm thalassocracy.
   Rhyme: the Vikings / the Hansa / the Atlantic powers.
7. **The industrial wave** → opening up of **BIF + coal** (from peat) → mass
   steel, machines.
8. **The present: the literal BEGINNING of the space age.** ← start of the
   story/game.

### The shift of axis and "archaeology training" `[decided]`
- The axis of seafaring: the warm inland sea (thalassocracy) → **the
  continent** (iron) → **the northern** sea (oceanic colonization). Two
  different seafaring traditions, separated by the continental Iron Age.
- The thalassocracy as readable archaeology is a **training ground** for the
  skill of reconstructing the past from ruins. The same skill then turns to
  the riddle of the seeding. Two layers of "studied but not remembered": the
  near one (the thalassocracy, 2–3 thousand years, readable) and the
  infinitely deep one (the seeding, ~1 million years, only the paradox + a
  point at L5).

### The space age — the story's present `[decided]`
- **The "present" = the literal beginning of the space age.** First orbits,
  stations, probes. **Resorts on Rhea and everyday settlement of the Pater
  system are the development horizon (the player's goal), NOT a starting
  given.**
- **Launching from Terra is noticeably EASIER than from Earth:** 0.7g +
  probably a thinner atmosphere → lower delta-v; in the rocket equation this is
  an **exponential** fuel gain → SSTO is realistic, early mass spaceflight.
  "Earth-level" chemical rockets are more than enough.
- **A two-phase structure (set by the geometry of "a moon in a giant's
  well"):**
  - *Phase 1 — "our own system":* easy launch + **cheap coastal shipping**
    between Pater's moons (intra-system transfers cost pennies in delta-v).
    This is where the first stations, the base on Rhea and mining are built;
    the first casualties of the radiation belts.
  - *Phase 2 — "going outward":* a **separate hard threshold** — breaking out
    of Pater's gravity well to heliocentric orbits (work against Pater's mass;
    0.7g does NOT help here). The second act: Eos, Chione, the expedition to
    the Ark at L5, the companion.
  - Contrast with Earth: Earth has one barrier (its own well) → straight to
    interplanetary flight; Terra has a low first barrier + **an extra stage**
    (Pater's well).
- **Radiation geography of the Pater system:** settlement is uneven, following
  the map of the belts. Rhea (a quiet zone) is the future "resort"; Io and the
  inner moons are deadly without heavy shielding (zones for armor/robots).
  Radiation reports = the "weather" of the era.
- **The Ark is discovered precisely now (one of the tracks, not the core):**
  only at the beginning of the space age is the faint +22 point at L5 **caught
  for the first time** by large optics — **a completely new, unknown object**
  (it was never seen before instruments; there is no myth about it). An
  anomalous artificial glint near Pater is a mystery. Mature science already
  holds the paleontological paradox; someone puts one next to the other. An
  expedition to the Ark is possible — **an interesting driver, but only one of
  several**; the setting does not require that anyone get there.
- **The arc of settlement (a ladder of thresholds), genre-neutral:** orbit and
  the Pater system (phase 1) → leaving Pater's well outward (phase 2) →
  settling outer targets. The outer targets are **equal**: Chione as a second
  home, Frater as a resource/fuel base, the belts, and — on equal footing —
  the discovery of and expedition to the Ark. The "third point" (terraforming
  Chione / a dash to the companion / a seeder of one's own) is a **possible**
  distant development, not an obligatory climax. Under different genres the
  ladder reads differently (development stages in a strategy game, missions in
  a space sim, goals in a sandbox, locations in an RPG).

## 7. Colonization and history

### The colonizer's mandate `[decided]`
- **Biology only, not geoengineering.** The task is to release a biosphere
  into a **climatically ready** world, not to change the climate. "Fly there
  and carefully release life in stages." Nothing fantastic apart from the very
  fact of a single sublight launch.
- This boundary determines which worlds are available to it:
  - **Terra — within the mandate:** already in the HZ, a liquid ocean, the
    right temperature. No need to change the climate. Terra's oxygenation is
    **biological** (organisms bury carbon), not abiotic — hence within the
    mandate; the cold climate arises as a biological **consequence**, not as
    geoengineering.
  - **Chione — outside the mandate:** it needs warming (geoengineering). Only
    a target for the descendants.
  - **The companion's planet — outside the mandate:** redder light (K spectrum
    versus the G primary) + not a "ready" world with a proven ocean. Known,
    but not guaranteed.
- **System selection criterion (at the senders' level):** one ready seedable
  world (Terra) **in a rich system** — a bet on the colony's future (Chione,
  the companion's planet, Frater's moons = room to grow). The criterion was
  correct (the ocean turned out to be where the spectrum promised); what
  failed was what a spectrum cannot measure — the stability of society.

### The Ark: architecture and mission `[decided]`
- **One** automated ship (one-shot). Deeply sublight
  (~3000 km/s, ~3500 years for ~35 light-years).
- **Propulsion and power — a fusion reactor that ran the WHOLE flight**
  (acceleration + cruise + deceleration). Magnetic confinement is a
  **persistent current in a superconductor** (the field is maintained **for
  free**, with no energy); the only costs are cryocooling of the loop + fusion
  fuel. Deceleration begins **well in advance** (a long low-thrust arc).
- **The Ark is a carrier-distributor, NOT a seeder.** Its mission = **deliver
  and drop seeder probes at N points** (possibly during a gravity-assist
  flyby of Pater — for correction and a convenient drop geometry). **The
  mission ends with the drop.**
- **Everything else is done LOCALLY by GROUND seeders**, each from its own
  landing point: deploying the biosphere, oxygenation, raising humans from
  embryos, hologram tutors, robots, "some sort of" manufacturing. It is **the
  probes themselves** (small, expendable, with their own thrust) that dive into
  Pater's well; the mothership stays outside.
- Explains: **N starting points**, the near/far schism, several language
  centers — and **the Ark's passivity** (after the drop it is a spent stage,
  not involved in the colony's collapse).
- **The ground seeders' manufacturing is "some sort of", FINITE, NOT a
  replicator** (a full self-reproducing factory was rejected as excessive).
  They deployed the minimum (incubators, nannies, holograms) → resources ran
  out → it stopped → a contribution to the degradation.
- **Arrived ~1–2 million years ago.** Biosphere: simple life → complex;
  oxygenation + assembly ~0.5–1.5 million years. **Humans were released ~100
  thousand years ago.**
- **No Stern-style telomere mechanism** (trivial biotechnology fixes
  telomerase — as a cause of decline it is false). The colonization
  **succeeded biologically**.

### The social failure `[decided]`
- The failure is **social**, not biological. "The village problem": the
  machine gestates and raises the body but **does not transmit culture**
  (culture lives only in living carriers; holograms/robots do not pass it on).
- The holograms' authority collapses **not from breakdown** but because people
  stop believing them (the second generation bonds with living, poorly
  socialized parents, not with machines).
- The children bootstrap a **new** culture themselves (precedents: Nicaraguan
  Sign Language, creole genesis) — but it is not Earth culture → "a departure
  from the plan".
- **This is a social catastrophe, not a design.** Machine upbringing failed to
  transmit culture — the holograms' authority collapsed, society fell apart
  and went feral. No hidden bottom: not a secret plan of the seeder, but a
  failure the creators could not foresee. (A variant "the divergence was
  intended" was considered earlier — **rejected**.)

### The deliberately severed link with Earth `[decided]`
- **The incoming channel (Earth→mission) was deliberately silenced**, soon
  after launch (~a few years), by decision of the creators — so that **no
  Earthly decisions could interfere** with the autonomous program. (Mechanism
  adapted from Stern, see below.)
- **Consequence — knowledge of Earth is frozen** at the moment "launch + a few
  years". Even the Ark's onboard archive (address, catalog, early history) is
  frozen at that point. The live channel is cut; the static archive is
  intact.
- **So, having read the Ark's memory, the descendants will get:** where the
  ancestral home was + what it was like at launch → they find it in the sky
  (rolling back stellar motion, see section 8). BUT **what became of Earth is
  a dead end; they will never learn it.** One can point a finger at the star
  and know nothing about its fate after the departure.
- Tone: Earth's fate is not a wound but a **deliberately inaccessible fact**,
  an intellectual block. The founders cut the thread themselves — not out of
  cruelty, but so that the program would not depend on what the metropole
  might degenerate into. The same overestimation of reliability as in the
  sociology (see "the single miscalculation" below).
- **Parallel with the source (Stern, adapted):** in Stern it is specifically
  **reception** that was silenced "for good" (dated 2195), while
  **transmission** to Earth went on for another ~12 thousand years; after ~20
  thousand years — a failed attempt at contact from the Solar System. We take
  the essence (the incoming channel cut deliberately for the sake of
  autonomy); whether outgoing transmission continues is an optional detail.

### The creators' single miscalculation `[decided]`
- The social failure (not transmitting culture) + the lack of long-term
  autonomy (no fight to stay operational — a bet on a **fast** rise of the
  colony, which would fix everything itself) + the deliberate cutting of the
  link (isolation as protection) — these are **one mistake**: overestimating
  how smoothly everything would go. The creators did not plan for a
  millennia-long failure and going feral. Thematically a single
  miscalculation across sociology, engineering and communication.

### Dogs `[decided]`
- Dogs genetically enhanced for cooperation — an **affective framework**
  (genuine attachment, co-regulation), which holograms lack.
  They solve the deprivation problem, **not** the culture problem.
- **NOT uplifted into a sapient successor species** (unlike Stern — a
  deliberate divergence). In our setting humans survived; no successor is
  needed.
- Co-evolving with humans for ~100 thousand years → no longer Earth dogs;
  sacred status is possible ("they were there at the creation").

### Languages and myths `[decided]`
- Over 100 thousand years (and even more so over a dozen cycles) —
  **complete divergence**: the horizon of the comparative method is ~6–10
  thousand years; this is an order of magnitude further. **Nothing** of the
  seeders' starting languages comes through. Languages arose endogenously
  (geography, near/far, climate).
- **Complexity inversion** (Trudgill, Lupyan & Dale): small isolated
  communities retain baroque grammar; large agrarian ones simplify it.
  → "savages" speak more complex languages than industrial peoples; grammar
  as an index of social scale.
- **Myths are not preserved as content** — BUT a structural archetype tied to
  a **permanent visible referent** survives 100 thousand years
  (phylogenetic mythology: "the Seven Sisters", "the Cosmic Hunt").
  A free-floating story perishes; a sky anchor does not.
- Terra has such an anchor: **Pater itself** (a huge "Eye" in the sky, ~9.5°,
  with a ring and eclipses once per "day") + the 3:2 partner, the small fry,
  the irregular. Stable sky archetypes are built around **the visible Pater**
  ("the Eye", "its ring", "the eye closes" for eclipses) — re-anchored by each
  epoch, because Pater is always visible to everyone.
- **The Ark plays NO part in mythology.** It is at Pater's L5, +22m —
  **invisible to the eye and to early optics**; pre-instrumental cultures
  **never observed** it. There is no ancient "myth of the Ark"; it enters
  culture only in the space age, as a **scientific discovery** (see sections
  7, 8).

### The pre-agrarian ceiling and the cycles of languages-and-cultures `[decided]`
- **Terra is the FIRST technological civilization.** In the ~100 thousand
  years since humans were released there was not a single technological
  takeoff — not because of cognitive immaturity (humans were fully capable the
  whole time; language is a deep, possibly million-year-old capacity), but
  because of an **environmental ceiling**.
- **Vinge's Slow Zone regime** shows up not as "they rose and fell
  technologically" but as **"they could not rise at all"** — a structural
  ceiling, not a catastrophe. In the ground — only pre-agrarian layers, ghost
  languages, and exactly one breakthrough (the current one).
- **The mechanism of the ceiling (threefold):**
  - *A catalog without an agrarian base.* The colonizer planted
    Carboniferous-type flora dispersed by spores, wind and water (an oxygen
    pump), not cereals. Cereals are present only in a few **tropical ranges**
    (from a few of the starting seeding points), but without the accompanying
    package they did not produce a takeoff — the presence of a cereal is
    necessary but not sufficient (the Earth norm).
  - *Climate.* A narrow warm belt, cold, a 7-day cycle with night frosts →
    farming lives as a ribbon near the equator and does not become a universal
    base.
  - *Geography of isolation.* Peat shores + tundra-and-snow continents + rocky
    coasts → the world is fragmented into **isolates**; there is no threshold
    density and exchange for a leap.
- **The ceiling holds through the FAILURE of repeated attempts**, not through
  their absence: sedentism tried to take hold in different pockets and failed
  every time (climatic reversal, exhaustion, death of the isolate).
  Archaeology → many proto-sedentary settlements that never fully developed.
- **"Cycles" = cycles of languages-and-cultures and of failed proto-sedentary
  settlements** under the ceiling (hundreds of ghost languages, the baroque
  grammar of isolates — Trudgill), NOT cycles of civilizations.
- **The breakthrough** (see 6-bis) is the first center that **did not fail**.
- **Hope:** knowing "this is the first colonization" leaves hope — "two points
  are not yet a verdict" (the method is proven; a third can be placed).

### Archaeology versus myth `[decided]`
- Terra's sterility **is readable by geology** long before spaceflight: a thin
  sedimentary cover (young), an abrupt break in the carbon isotope signature,
  a steep oxygen front in the rock, crater-age antiquity against biological
  youth.
- **The paradox rests on kinship-without-a-tree and on clocks, NOT on the
  absence of human ancestors.** The biosphere is complete and rich (see
  section 6, "Catalog and endemics"): hundreds of thousands of species were
  carried over, including terrestrial primates and human relatives. So "there
  are no transitional forms to humans" is **wrong** — they exist. The paradox
  lies elsewhere:
  - **A tree with a severed root:** the clades are related by the Earth code,
    but the whole tree **has no prebiotic foundation on this planet** and no
    connecting links *between* the major clades (ready-made branches were
    carried over, not grown from a common trunk here).
  - **Molecular clocks (millions to billions of years of clade depth) versus
    stratigraphy (thousands of years of sediment)** — a discrepancy of orders
    of magnitude that no cataclysm can remove. This is the main "irreducible
    residue".
- Two prescientific hypotheses of the descendants — both **wrong**: "an
  explosion of life" (stumbles on the clocks and the severed root) and "a lost
  ancestral home / a drowned continent" (the clocks do not fit into the thin
  sediment of the whole planet). The truth (the seeding) becomes accessible no
  earlier than they grow into the optics / an expedition to the Ark — and this
  is **one of** the scientific storylines, not an obligatory one.

## 8. The Ark at L5 and the lost address `[decided]`

### Position and visibility
- A derelict at **Pater's L5** (60° behind Pater along its solar orbit) —
  ~0.85 AU from Terra (~1.3×10¹¹ m).
- **Why L5 specifically:** a decelerating ship arrives on a heliocentric
  trajectory and **physically cannot** dive into Pater's deep well and climb
  back out (no delta-v budget). L5 is the **natural terminus** of a
  decelerating heliocentric craft: a gravitationally stable parking spot with
  no fuel needed, outside the well/magnetosphere/radiation, with a view. The
  most the Ark could do at Pater was a **gentle gravity-assist flyby**
  (correction + a convenient geometry for dropping the probes). It was **the
  probes themselves** that dived into the well.
- Geometry: a tadpole orbit, stable over geological time (Pater/star mass
  ratio ~3×10⁻⁴ ≪ the 0.0385 threshold).
- **Brightness ~+22 magnitude** (a thin strand 2 km × ~20 m, albedo 0.2).
  Invisible to the eye (limit +6), invisible to early optics (Herschel's
  reflectors ~+14). **Consequence: pre-instrumental cultures NEVER saw it** →
  there is no myth of the Ark; it enters culture only in the space age, as a
  **new scientific discovery** (large optics / photographic plate).
- Mythology is anchored to **the visible Pater** ("the Eye", the ring,
  eclipses), not to the invisible Ark (see the section on languages and
  myths).
- A high area-to-mass ratio → radiation pressure slowly pushes the derelict
  out of ideal L5 (usable: a dead, creeping beacon).

### The ship's current state `[decided]`
- **Fully passive mode.** The mission ended with the probe drop; fighting to
  stay operational was not planned for (a bet on a fast rise of the colony —
  see "the single miscalculation").
- **The fusion reactor is irreversibly dead.** On completion it was shut down
  normally → active cryocooling stopped → the superconducting loop warmed above
  Tc → lost superconductivity. A restart is impossible: to start it, the loop
  must be cooled; to cool it, reactor power is needed (chicken and egg).
  An RTG cannot start it — that needs megawatts, and an RTG gives watts.
- **The RTG watchman is alive.** A long-lived isotope (²³⁸U/²³²Th, will not
  run down in a million years) → powers **minimal electronics** (memory,
  timers). Not propulsion, not a starter. "Something is still ticking" a
  million years later.
- **The sunshade is deployed and passively cools** to ~40–60 K. Two
  thresholds: enough for the embryos (~77 K) (preserved), not enough for the
  superconductor (<10–18 K) (the reactor is dead). One shield saves the
  embryos and does not save the reactor.
- **The shade is DEGRADING** (micrometeoroid erosion over ~1–2 million years →
  heat inflow grows, T creeps up) → **part of the embryo stock has possibly
  already died**, but what remains is **recoverable — an engineering task**.
- **What is at L5:**
  - **The main embryo bank was SPENT** — it went into the ground seeder
    probes.
  - **Spare probes were HELD BACK:** the first wave took root normally → the
    need disappeared → they were kept "just in case", not dropped. With an
    embryo stock (partly degraded), recoverable.
  - **The data archive** — address, catalog, log; frozen at "launch + a few
    years" (the link was cut, see section 7).
- **The material prize of an expedition (if anyone gets there):** not "a
  revelation about origins" but **recoverable spare seeder probes + data** —
  a literal **means of placing the "third point"** (seeding the companion's
  empty Terra): repair the probes → refuel at Frater → deliver to the
  companion. A concrete toolkit, not philosophy. The Ark holds at L5 a means
  it never used itself.

### Stellar drift — the lost address
- The original distance to the ancestral home was ~30–40 light-years.
- Over 1–2 million years the **relative** drift of neighboring stars
  (~20–40 km/s) → **~100–200 light-years** of separation. The original 30–40
  is within the error. The ancestral home has been carried hundreds of
  light-years away, into another part of the arm.
- **The sky has not preserved the address:** constellations live ~10⁵ years;
  this is one to two orders of magnitude longer → the pattern is completely
  rewritten. There is no pointer "we came from there". The ancestral home is
  **unfindable**, not merely unreachable.
- **The Ark carries the address, and it is READABLE `[decided]`:** its memory
  preserves the original coordinates, the launch date, the arrival vector.
  Decoding the Ark + **rolling stellar motion backward** (knowing the vector
  and the drift time, the kinematics can be unwound) → the ancestral home is
  **eventually found in the sky**. The address erased by drift is restored —
  a navigational prize, unique in the universe. (This is one of the possible
  story tracks, not an obligatory ending.)

### Tone: no homesickness `[decided]`
- **There is no longing** — no one can long for a place that no ancestor ever
  saw. Terra is the homeland; Earth is a hypothesis from a scientific paper.
- The grandeur of the design does not provoke pathos: even at the start of the
  space age, origin is not a shrine but a disputed scientific fact. (Everyday
  space settlement — the Pater system, "a vacation on Rhea" — is the
  **development horizon**, not the present; but the psychology of
  indifference to origins is already in place.)
- **Epistemic asymmetry:**
  - *Scientists* hold the solution as an academic result → **research
    excitement**, not reverence. "It fits together beautifully; and that +22
    point over there, if we fly to it, carries the coordinates."
  - *Mass society* — **rational skepticism** (an extraordinary claim, weak
    accessible proof, against it common sense and myths).
    "So it happened, so what."
- **The attitude toward the Ark is investigative, not sentimental:** if anyone
  flies to it, it is as an expedition for data, not a pilgrimage. (This is the
  tone of *one track*, not the genre of the whole setting — see section 11.)
- **The hope is not "we will go home" but "the method is proven, we can do it
  ourselves"**: an interstellar dash works (Terra is living proof) → the
  pragmatic next step is to place a third point. Not out of longing, out of
  cold curiosity.
- **The setting's register:** no pathos of origin — no cult of seeder
  ancestors, no trauma of abandonment, no awe before Earth. A fact, a dispute
  about the fact, a handful of the curious. An indifferent universe and
  curious people.

## 9. Frater — the super-Jupiter and fuel hub `[decided]`

### Frater itself `[decided]`
- **2–3 M_J** (confidently a planet, far from the deuterium threshold of
  ~13 M_J), orbit **circular, 5–7 AU** (circular → the secular pumping of
  Pater's/Terra's e vanishes, the climate is safe).
- Beyond the snow line → cold, ice is stable.
- Self-luminous in the IR (youth), but by 2 Gyr the heat has faded.
- Retinue budget at 2–3 M_J ≈ 0.1 M⊕ — ~10× more than Pater's.

### The retinue — "normal", in contrast to lopsided Pater `[decided]`
- **2–3 large icy moons** of Ganymede/Callisto class in a **Laplace resonant
  chain** (analogue of Io-Europa-Ganymede 4:2:1): an even mass distribution,
  disk-formed, coplanar. There was no captured dominant → the retinue is
  **even** (like Jupiter's), setting off Pater's lopsidedness ("dominant +
  swarm").
- **Subsurface oceans** under icy crusts — a **physical inevitability** (tidal
  heating from the resonance pumps e). The innermost moon is possibly a
  lava-ice transitional case (Europa→Io).
- A swarm of small icy bodies + **a rich system of bright ICY rings** (Frater
  is cold, ice is stable → bright icy rings — a contrast to Pater's dark
  refractory ones).
- Division of ecologies: Pater — warm, dry, rocky-and-lava, one wet world;
  Frater — cold, icy, an even retinue, oceans under the crust.

### Native life — just a "mystery", a detail `[OPEN, deliberately]`
- The conditions for life (a liquid ocean + tidal energy + time) **exist**;
  whether there is **life** is unknown and **unverifiable** within the game's
  horizon (an ocean under tens of km of ice on a moon at 5–7 AU). The
  characters physically cannot know.
- **This is just an item of description, a local "mystery"** — with no
  thematic weight and no role in the story. We do not load it (analogous to
  our own question about Europa/Enceladus: interesting, but background). Open
  in any direction for the future.

### Role — the logistics hub of the second act `[decided]`
- **IMPORTANT — Frater's well is deep** (2–3 M_J). "Refueling at Frater" is
  NOT justified by "shallow wells" (that was a mistake): lifting fuel out of
  Frater's gravity is comparable in delta-v to launching from Terra. The hub
  rests on other arguments:
  - **³He for the long leg.** Fusion fuel (D/³He) is present in the
    atmospheres of **both** giants (hydrogen-helium envelopes). BUT **Pater
    is warm** (0.85 AU) → fractionation/escape of light isotopes depleted its
    ³He, plus a radiation-heavy environment (belts + active star). **Frater is
    cold** (5–7 AU) → its atmosphere retained ³He better; it is **relatively
    richer**. Hence: the early phase flies on **Pater's** ³He (nearby, at
    home, enough for intra-system flights); the long dash to the companion —
    on **Frater's** rich ³He.
  - **The rings — ice without a well.** Rich icy rings = reaction mass in
    orbit; **no need to land/take off**. The best source of reaction mass.
  - **A dead world can be milked industrially** (Terra — inhabited — cannot).
- **Reaction mass** (not "fuel"): water from the rings/outer moons → H₂+O₂ by
  electrolysis, or water directly. The base logically goes on an **outer**
  moon (the edge of the well) or works directly off the rings.
- **³He from a giant's atmosphere** is a **late, heavy technology** (platforms
  in gravity/radiation, isotope separation). Two stages by source:
  Pater (early, nearby, poor) → Frater (late, farther, rich).
- **Frater is a MANDATORY hub of the long leg** (not a fuel monopolist!):
  the dash to the companion needs **refueling halfway**, and Frater is the
  only point on that leg where rich ³He and reaction mass (rings/moons) are in
  one place, and beyond which there is **nothing** all the way to the
  companion (no disk). Arc: the Pater system (Pater's ³He) → going outward →
  refueling at Frater (rich ³He + reaction mass) → the dash to the companion.

### No scattered disk — a consequence of binarity `[decided]`
- **There is NO scattered disk / Oort cloud:** the companion (100–200 AU) +
  tidal truncation sweep out/destabilize the far periphery. There is no outer
  cold reservoir of small icy bodies in free space.
- Consequence: **there is no distributed source of volatiles** → reaction mass
  is **concentrated in hubs**, the main one being the Frater system. You
  cannot collect ice "along the way" — only "reach the hub, load up". This is
  what makes Frater the mandatory **refueling stop of the long leg**: beyond it
  there is nothing all the way to the companion (no volatiles, no ³He), so the
  full tank is taken on there.
- **Comets are few**, and they are driven by **the giants' internal dynamics**
  (Frater + the Neptune-like gatekeeper through resonant mixing of the
  truncated belt) — NOT by the companion (at an inclination ≤15° Kozai–Lidov is
  switched off; the companion is inert). Rare, irregular comets from a meager
  residual belt; the system is cometarily quiet (consistent with "few comets":
  no reservoir + sluggish internal sources + the flux declining as the system
  matures; the young flux was higher).
- Binarity is a **scarcity driver along two axes:** the compactness of the
  companion's system (truncated disk) + the absence of a distributed source of
  volatiles. (The rarity of comets is about the meager belt and sluggish
  sources, and NOT about the companion: at low inclination it does not take
  part dynamically.)

## 9-ter. The ice giant ("gatekeeper") — Frater's "stunted twin" `[decided]`
> The label "gatekeeper" stuck during discussion; after the companion's low
> inclination was fixed, its function is **master of the outer belt** (not a
> regulator of comet showers, which do not come via the companion).
- **One ice giant of ~10–15 M⊕ at ~15 AU** (between Frater at 7 and the
  truncated edge at ~45–60). Stable there; beyond ~20 AU it is already shaky
  (the companion tugs at it).
- **Origin — a failed core, not "an ordinary Neptune".** An "ordinary" Neptune
  at 15 AU is poorly motivated: ice giants on wide orbits grow slowly (little
  material), and Frater nearby also eats up the zone. Solution: during
  Frater's formation there were **two core embryos** in the same zone; one won
  the gas accretion → **Frater** (gas giant), the other stalled at an icy mass
  below the runaway threshold → **this Neptune**. Frater's failed twin from
  the same material.
- **The orbit is circular and coplanar** (born in the disk, not ejected → not
  eccentric).
- **Role — master of the outer belt:** sculpts the structure of the truncated
  belt with its resonances, captures trojans, **supplies the few comets there
  are** (through resonant mixing together with Frater — like Jupiter/Neptune
  in the Solar System). There is **no need** to regulate comet showers from the
  companion — at low inclination (≤15°) Kozai–Lidov is switched off, and there
  are no showers via the companion.
- **The thematic rhyme "almost, but fell short":** the stunted giant next to
  Frater ↔ the stunted home Chione ↔ (optionally) Terra's stunted
  civilization. "Almost" as a running motif.
- Resource-wise it duplicates Frater (volatiles, ³He), but is farther and less
  accessible → secondary as a fuel hub; its main function is dynamical.

## 9-bis. The companion star and its system `[decided / details open]`

### The companion star `[decided]`
- **A wide binary**, separation **~100–200 AU** (midpoint ~150). Born together
  from a common cloud → the companion has **its own full disk**. Half of
  Sun-like stars are in multiple systems — not exotic.
- **Early K (~0.8 M☉, K0–K2)**, L ≈ **0.3–0.4 L☉**, T_eff ~5000 K. Yellower and
  redder than the main G star, but not "red". Flare activity at 2 Gyr is
  moderate (not an M dwarf).
- **Small mutual orbital inclination — ≤15°.** A common large disk +
  fragmentation → correlated axes. Statistics of wide pairs: at the close end
  of the range (100–200 AU) alignment is common (wider → more random
  orientations; here — the boundary, low inclination is plausible).
- Name — `[OPEN]` (for now "the Companion").
- **Does not disturb the inner system:** the primary's planets are stable out
  to ~1/5 of periastron ≈ 20–40 AU; everything that matters (out to Frater at
  7 AU) is inside with margin. It does not perturb Terra/Chione/Frater.
- **Dynamically INERT.** At an inclination ≤15° **Kozai–Lidov is switched
  off** (it needs >~39°) → the companion barely tugs at the system, **there
  are no comet showers via it**. That is fine — not every object has to
  perturb things. It strengthens "settled vs untouched": it just hangs there
  with its poor system, influencing nothing.
- **Terra's sky:** a dazzling yellow-white point of **~−13 magnitude**
  (brighter than the full Moon), but precisely a point — no disk and no warmth
  (~0.007 W/m²). Not "a second sun" but a permanent shining beacon, creeping
  over the centuries. At low inclination it moves **almost along the
  ecliptic**, in the plane of the system (part of the plane, not a stranger to
  it). It points to where "that other world" is.

### The companion's system — poor `[decided]`
- **Poorer than the main one**, and naturally so: the companion is **less
  massive** (0.8 vs 0.92 M☉) + its disk is **tidally truncated** by the binary
  to ~0.3–0.4 of the separation = **~45–60 AU** → less material, less time →
  fewer planets. Not impoverishment for the plot's sake — a consequence of
  physics.
- **One gas giant** (Jupiter/Saturn class, ~2–4 AU, beyond the compressed snow
  line at ~1.6 AU) — the "organizer" of the poor system.
- **Several solid planets** inside + an HZ world.
- **No distant ice giants** (truncated), a meager belt, a **truncated edge** at
  ~45–60 AU (a geometric signature of a binary).
- L ≈ 0.35 L☉ **shifts everything inward**: HZ ~0.55 AU (belt 0.45–0.75),
  snow line ~1.6 AU. A compressed version.
- Layout (∝ √0.35, compressed):
  - **Inner rocky planets** (~0.2–0.4 AU): 1–2 scorched worlds under reddish
    light.
  - **HZ planet (~0.55 AU)** — the target of the second act, see below.
  - **Gas giant** (~2–4 AU) — the system's only giant.
  - **A meager belt** at the snow line / the giant's resonances.
  - **Truncated edge** at ~45–60 AU.
- **Contrast of the systems:** the main one is rich and complex (two giants +
  the Neptune-like gatekeeper + Chione + Terra-with-moons + Eos + belts); the
  companion's is modest and cramped (one giant + a handful of solid planets +
  an empty Terra). "Settled vs untouched" reads even at the level of star
  systems.

### The companion's HZ planet — "the empty Terra" `[decided]`
- **A sterile, ready analogue of Terra:** Earth-like, in the HZ, with water and
  the right climate, **but without life** (abiogenesis did not happen / did not
  have time — the system is young). Not tidally locked (the K HZ is far from an
  M HZ) — normal days.
- **KEY — the atmosphere is Archean, anoxic:** free O₂ is a product of a
  biosphere; without life the atmosphere is reducing (N₂/CO₂/CH₄). Not
  breathable. The planet is literally in the state in which Terra met the Ark.
- **"They could have chosen it too":** for the senders it was an **equally
  valid target** (also a ready world). What tipped the choice to Terra:
  - Terra orbits the **G primary** (native Earth light); the companion's
    planet orbits a K star (redder) → an Earth biosphere takes root more
    reliably under G.
  - Terra's ocean was **confirmed** spectroscopically; the companion's planet
    remained a **candidate** (the star is drowned in the primary's glare, the
    signal is dirtier).
  - One shot → take the world with fewer unknowns, leave the equally valid one
    "for later". **Known to the senders**; the record is in the Ark's memory.
- **Thematic role = a mirror of the first act.** On arrival, the descendants
  find **an empty Terra, ready for life**. This is the tangible "third point":
  the terraforming task is exactly the one the colonizer solved (releasing an
  oxygen pump into an Archean world) — the descendants **repeat the seeding
  from scratch**, already knowing how it ended for themselves (including the
  open question: will they also repeat the social failure). It closes the
  theme of hope into a concrete object: "there stands an empty Terra — go and
  seed it".

## 11. Story drivers — equal

The setting is a **sandbox space**, not a line leading to a single secret.
The drivers below are **equal**; origin/the Ark is one of them, not the core.
Under different genres they read differently (stages in a strategy game,
missions in a space sim, goals in a sandbox, locations/quests in an RPG).

- **Settling the Pater system (phase 1).** Orbit, stations, coastal shipping
  between the moons, a base on Rhea (a "resort" — a goal, not a given),
  mining. The radiation geography of the belts as "weather".
- **Leaving Pater's well outward (phase 2).** A hard technological threshold
  in its own right — an engineering/economic driver.
- **Resources and logistics.** Pater's ³He (early) → Frater as the fuel hub of
  the long leg (³He + reaction mass from the rings). The fuel economy of the
  whole expansion.
- **Chione — a second home.** A terraformable (easier than Mars) cold world;
  colonization/terraforming as a distant development goal.
- **Frater.** A resource base + icy moons/oceans + a local "mystery" of life
  (background).
- **Society and politics of a young spacefaring civilization.** The
  thalassocracy in archaeology, the continental Iron Age and the northern
  colonization — living historical memory; a Nordic maritime character; the
  near/far schism as a cultural axis.
- **Origin and the Ark (one of several, not the core).** The paleontological
  paradox → the seeding hypothesis → discovery of the Ark by optics → a
  possible expedition to L5 (the address of the ancestral home + a lead to the
  companion's planet). The setting works without the reveal too.
- **The far horizon.** The "third point": a dash to the companion (an empty
  Terra, ready for seeding), a seeder of one's own sent outward. Possible, not
  obligatory.

**Why seeding rather than a native civilization (a note on the setting's
intent):** the seeding is a **tool** for having **a recognizable Earth
biosphere in a young system** (2 Gyr — it would not have had time on its own),
not for building the story around "where we came from". A native civilization
would give a coherent tree without paradoxes, but would require an old system
and alien biochemistry (everything unfamiliar, less depth of contrast). An
Earth biosphere gone wild + a mystery in the background is richer, and the
mystery remains a **detail**, not a mechanism.
